# HYPERION — Story Bible (Dan Simmons, 1989; first novel only)

A factual reference for a faithful point-and-click adaptation. It covers the first novel *Hyperion* only. *The Fall of Hyperion* (1990) and later books are excluded on purpose. Where a later book reveals something that changes how a *Hyperion* detail should be read, it is listed in §8 under "do NOT use", so the game does not spoil it or contradict the first book.

Everything below is paraphrased. The only verbatim text is a handful of very short phrases, each marked as a quote.

## 0. Sources and confidence key

**Confidence key** (used on every section and on many single facts):

- **[HIGH]**: checked against the novel's own text, using the official German publisher reading sample of *Die Hyperion-Gesänge* (Heyne/Penguin Random House DE, a translation of the Prologue, Part 1 and the start of Part 2), or agreed on by two or more independent secondary sources.
- **[MED]**: from one solid secondary source (fan-wiki pages that cite page numbers, or a study guide), not contradicted elsewhere, and consistent with the researcher's recollection of the book.
- **[LOW]**: from a single weak source, or from recollection only. Check against a physical copy before you build a puzzle or scene on it.

**Sources used**

1. Official reading sample, Heyne "Die Hyperion-Gesänge" (PDF, prestelpublishing.penguinrandomhouse.de, leseprobe_9783453529786). It contains the Prologue, the whole Yggdrasill dinner, the lot-drawing, the whole Priest's Tale, the Consul and Hoyt scene, and the landing at Keats. This is the primary text source for §1 (Prologue, Part 1) and §3.1.
2. Hyperion Cantos Fandom wiki (hyperioncantos.fandom.com), read through its API: "Hyperion Plot Summary", "Final Shrike Pilgrimage" (cites 1995 Bantam page numbers), and the character and location pages.
3. English Wikipedia: "Hyperion (Simmons novel)" and "Hyperion Cantos".
4. Shmoop *Hyperion* study guide: Prologue, chapter 1–6 and Epilogue summaries, and character analyses (these quote the novel with chapter.paragraph references).
5. BookRags *Hyperion* chapter abstracts, chapters 1–8 (free excerpts only).
6. Miscellany of Fiction chapter summary; FunTrivia *Hyperion* quiz; techno-science.net (a French mirror of the Wikipedia article on secondary characters).

**Structural note.** The novel has a Prologue, six numbered chapters and an Epilogue. Each chapter holds frame narration plus one pilgrim's tale. (The German edition labels the chapters "Erster Teil", "Zweiter Teil" and so on.) The frame is told in close third person, usually from the Consul's point of view. The Consul's own name is never given.

### 1.1 Prologue [HIGH: official sample]

- **Location.** The Consul sits on the extendable balcony of his ebony spaceship, playing Rachmaninoff's Prelude in C-sharp minor on an ancient, well-kept **Steinway**. Below, big green saurian creatures crowd and bellow in the swamps and wander into the ship's containment (repulsion) field. A storm is building in the north: a forest of 200-metre gymnosperms, bruise-black clouds and lightning. Indigo mist. The world is never named; the Consul thinks of himself as the only sentient being on it. He has spent his days hunting big game in the fern forests with a Vikken EMV.
- **Message.** The fatline receiver chimes. The Consul pours a scotch and plays the message in the ship's holo niche. CEO **Meina Gladstone** speaks from Tau Ceti Center. Her points:
  - He has been chosen for the Shrike Pilgrimage by the Church of the Shrike, and the choice has been confirmed by the All Thing.
  - Three standard weeks ago the Time Tombs appeared to be opening. The anti-entropic fields are expanding fast, and the Shrike now roams as far south as the Bridle Range.
  - A FORCE:space task force has left Parvati to evacuate Hyperion's Hegemony citizens. Its time-debt is a little over three Hyperion years.
  - An Ouster migration cluster ("Swarm") of at least 4,000 units is heading for the system. A full FORCE battle fleet with a farcaster construction battalion has been detached from the Camn System.
  - The Templars are sending their treeship **Yggdrasill**. He can reach it with three weeks of time-debt before it jumps from the Parvati system. The other six pilgrims will be aboard.
  - Intelligence says at least one of the seven is an Ouster agent, identity unknown. Sol Weintraub and Fedmahn Kassad are named as pilgrims.
  - If the Ousters take Hyperion, their agent must be eliminated and the Tombs sealed at any cost.
- **The Consul's reaction.** When the ship asks whether to reply, he says no. In bed under the skylight he plays Wagner's "Ride of the Valkyries" against the storm. He remembers ice crystals blowing through the ruins near the Tombs, the steel of the Shrike's impossible tree of metal thorns, screams in the night, and the Shrike's many-faceted ruby-and-blood gaze. This hints that he has been to the Tombs himself.
- **Backstory.** He spent 11 years as Hegemony Consul on Hyperion. In that time the Shrike Church sent a dozen barges of offworld pilgrims to the Tombs, and none returned.
- **Departure.** Before dawn the ship rises on a column of blue plasma toward the rendezvous.

### 1.2 Chapter 1, aboard the Yggdrasill [HIGH: official sample]

- **Waking.** The Consul wakes from cryogenic fugue in a small, windowless, egg-shaped environment pod (transparent walls, dark grass carpet, curved weirwood struts). Two very small crew clones are present. One offers him the traditional post-fugue glass of orange juice. Captain **Het Masteen**, the hooded "True Voice of the Tree", tells him the tree is two light-minutes and five hours from Hyperion. Being woken by the captain personally is a great honour.
- **The Consul's ship** has been taken out of storage, refuelled, and is waiting in **Sphere 11**, one of the cargo and engine spheres at the tree's base. The pilgrims have agreed to land in it if FORCE allows. A Hegemony warship is escorting them.
- **Walk to dinner.** They climb railless walkways around the trunk. There is a 600 m drop, and "down" is a sixth of standard gravity made by singularities caged in the tree's base. They cross a fragile suspension bridge to a 5 m-thick branch and ride a basket lift on a braided carbon cable up into the foliage. The tree is suspiciously empty: the six pilgrims are its only passengers, though a treeship normally carries 2,000–5,000. The Consul reflects that the Yggdrasill is "one of only five of its kind" and that bringing it into a war zone is a huge risk for the Templar Brotherhood.
- **Dinner** is on the **captain's dining platform**: a long wooden table at one end of a wide platform, stars overhead, and a solid sphere of foliage curving around like the green rind of a giant fruit. Masteen sits at the head and the Consul sits on his left. Masteen introduces everyone (descriptions are in §4). Asked whether Weintraub's baby is the seventh pilgrim, Masteen says no: only someone able to choose consciously to seek the Shrike can count. **He is the seventh.** Everyone knows that the Shrike Church only lets groups with a **prime number** of pilgrims travel north. Crew clones serve the last meal before planetfall: wine; roast beef and boiled sky squid (Shmoop) [MED]; Lamia eats the last of the chocolate cheesecake [HIGH].
- **Talk at dinner.** The Ousters are not yet in the system; fusion skirmishes have been detected in the Oort cloud. Each pilgrim gives a religious affiliation. Silenus reels off a comic list of faiths ending in "simple pagan". Lamia says she ignores religions. Masteen calls the belief that the Shrike is an avatar of punishment a heresy. They recall the legend that the Shrike grants one pilgrim's wish and kills the rest.
- **Proposal and vote.** See §2.
- **The tale.** Hoyt fetches two small, stained, fire-scorched leather notebooks: Paul Duré's journals. He says that to tell his own story he must tell another man's. Sol sets his sleeping week-old daughter on a padded mat beside his chair and runs white noise from his comlog. Masteen pulls his hood forward. Sol lights a pipe. Silenus leans in and recites a few lines of Chaucer's *Canterbury Tales* (the passage where the storytelling game is opened) [MED: the sample quotes it in German translation]. The tale is told at the table while Hyperion grows to fill a third of the sky.
- **After the tale.** Masteen tells everyone to gather with their luggage at the Consul's ship in Sphere 11 within 30 minutes. He will take one of the treeship's own dropships down separately and meet them at the **Shrike Temple in Keats** before their midnight departure. He gives the Consul a sealed comlog token that also works as a master key. In the ship's lounge Kassad runs a hand over the Steinway and asks if it is a harpsichord; the Consul tells him it is a pre-Hegira piano.
- **Hoyt's quarters.** Hoyt's pod is halfway up the tree on a secondary branch. The Consul finds him writhing on the grass carpet; his ultramorphine injector has jammed. The Consul loads a fresh injector and withholds it until Hoyt tells "the rest of the story" (see §3.1). He then injects him in the armpit and opens Hoyt's shirt. There is one cruciform on the chest and a second, smaller one between the shoulder blades. The Consul dresses the unconscious priest and carries him to the ship himself; he weighs almost nothing. He is reminded of carrying a sleeping son to bed, because the Consul was once the father of a child who is now dead.

### 1.3 Chapter 2, landing at Keats; the Benares [HIGH for the landing (sample); MED for the rest]

- **Landing.** It is a warm, rainy evening at Keats, the capital. A double sonic boom, then the Consul's ebony ship drops through the clouds on fusion flame. Ruby landing beams from the spaceport, which lies north of the city, guide it into a blast pit. Water jets cool it. A balcony extends and five pilgrims step out (Hoyt is still unconscious).
- **Arrival details.** Silenus wears a long purple cape and a red beret slouched to the right shoulder, and has a wine glass in hand. Rachel wakes and cries. The Consul has been away eight local years. The spaceport used to be 9 km from town and saw one ship a week. Now it is ringed by shacks, tents and mud streets, with 20-plus craft on the field, new blast pits, military modules and antenna farms. Thousands of people press against the perimeter fence and a violet containment field, guarded by FORCE:Marines over mined or deathbeam-covered ground: everyone wants off-planet. Across the river to the south, the rough-carved stone face of **Sad King Billy** looms on a hill. Silenus, drunk, delivers a mock "Alas, poor Yorick" over it.
- **Theo Lane** [MED], the Consul's former aide and now Governor-General, meets them. He explains:
  - Hyperion has been brought into the Hegemony Protectorate.
  - Keats is swollen with refugees.
  - The Shrike is appearing ever farther from the Tombs.
  - War is coming.

  Theo urges the Consul to give up the pilgrimage and offers him the governor-generalship (BookRags). The Consul refuses.
- **Through the city** [MED]. They ride a skimmer south toward Jacktown (Old City). The great **Shrike Temple** has been reduced to rubble by shaped plasma charges during riots against the Church. They go on to **Cicero's**, the Consul's old haunt in Jacktown. It is packed, and the owner, **Stan Leweski**, gives them a private room upstairs. After the meal the android **A. Bettik** comes for them.
- **The Benares** [MED]. They rendezvous with **Het Masteen** at the Shrike Temple and board the barge **Benares** around midnight. A. Bettik and an android crew run it. It is an old river barge (a former levitation barge; Fandom calls it the "levitation barge Benares") hauled upstream by harnessed **river mantas**. They head north-north-east up the **Hoolie River**.
- **Next morning** [MED]. Over breakfast they talk about Silenus's age; he is old enough to remember Old Earth. A. Bettik remarks (quoted by Shmoop) that androids are free of original sin and so exempt from the Shrike's retribution. **Kassad tells his tale on the Benares after breakfast.**
- **After the tale** [MED]:
  - They ask whether Moneta is the Shrike. Kassad does not know.
  - Asked whether he saw any of the present pilgrims on the Tree of Thorns, he says yes, but will not say who.
  - Hoyt asks whether he thinks he is meant to start the interstellar war. Kassad says yes.
  - Asked what he will ask the Shrike for, he says he means to kill it and Moneta.

### 1.4 Chapter 3, Naiad [MED]

- The Benares stops at the river port of **Naiad**, once home to about 20,000 people. It has been burned to ashes, including its small riverfront Shrike temple, its Customhouse, the River Front Inn (a famous boarding house from Sad King Billy's day) and the Airship Terminal. The Hyperion Self-Defense Force destroyed it while trying to contain the Shrike. Lamia asks A. Bettik why nobody knew. He says communications are down.
- Farther upriver they see no sign of civilisation. The Fandom wiki lists places upriver of Naiad: Betty's Ford, which has a cross-river ferry, and Cave Point, home of the River Runners Inn. Use these as passing scenery.
- That evening, at dinner on the Benares, Silenus says it is his turn (he drew the next number) and tells his tale.

### 1.5 Chapter 4, Edge, the Sea of Grass, the windwagon [MED; Yggdrasill's destruction HIGH by several sources]

- **Edge.** The Benares reaches **Edge**, a small deserted outpost at the head of the Hoolie on the south-western edge of the Sea of Grass. They see abandoned buildings from the dock. A. Bettik walks with them to the top of a low bluff and then takes his leave. The androids are now free to follow their own "purposes and pilgrimages" on Hyperion, and he and the crew take the Benares back downriver.
- **Waiting.** The windwagon is not there yet. As night falls the pilgrims build a **bonfire** so it can find them.
- **What each brought.** Silenus points out that the key to their salvation may be somewhere in their pile of luggage:
  - Silenus: his manuscript.
  - Kassad: his weapons.
  - The Consul: his spaceship, which can be called by remote. This is hinted rather than admitted.
  - Het Masteen: a heavy **Möbius cube**, a container for dangerous cargo. He will not say what is inside. Masteen also says that salvation may be found at the Tombs.

  Sol offers to exchange contact details with Masteen, who declines.
- **Lamia and Silenus.** Their hostility comes to a head: Lamia pulls a weapon on Silenus, and Sol mildly reminds her there is a child present (Shmoop quotes the line).
- **The windwagon** arrives on its own, with no crew aboard. The galley is stocked and the Consul cooks dinner. **Sol tells his tale over dinner on the windwagon.** Afterwards he invites everyone up on deck for air.
- **Night.** They set watches. In the sky they see the first fighting between the Hegemony and the Ousters in Hyperion orbit, and a burning point of light that is the **Yggdrasill** being destroyed. Het Masteen says nothing and goes below, visibly distraught. Kassad hands the Consul a deathwand for his watch.

### 1.6 Chapter 5, Masteen vanishes; Pilgrims' Rest; the tram [MED]

- **Morning.** Breakfast is found ready, but **Het Masteen is missing**. His cabin is spattered with blood, and he left no message. They split up and search the windwagon and his belongings: no body. The Möbius cube is found unopened. The Consul guesses it holds an **erg**, a force-field-generating creature the Templars can communicate with, which Masteen meant to use against the Shrike. Silenus suggests the Shrike has started "harvesting" them. Hoyt seems in shock and later helps carry Masteen's luggage. Lamia sums up that they know nothing except that Masteen is gone and the blood is there (paraphrase of a line Shmoop quotes). Masteen would have told his tale next. Lamia, who is after him, wants to wait until they are off the windwagon.
- **Pilgrims' Rest** is a small port town on the north-east shore at the foot of the **Bridle Range**. Even before it was abandoned it was little more than hovels and cheap hotels. Now it is empty, cold and snowing.
- **The tramway** crosses the Bridle Range to Chronos Keep:
  - 192 duralloy and whiskered-carbon pylons, each 83 m high, over a 96 km route.
  - It passes over the summit of **Mount Dryden**, at 9,246 m the fifth-highest peak in a range whose highest peaks top 9,000 m (Fandom, citing 1995 Bantam pp. 320–325).
  - The cars used to be kept moving constantly so the cables stayed flexed and free of ice. That has stopped.

  **Kassad gets the system running** and barely makes it back aboard. The crossing takes about **12 hours**.
- **In the tramcar**, after dinner, **Lamia tells her tale.** Afterwards Kassad asks whether Het Masteen could have been the Templar Johnny met. She says it is possible but she never found out. Kassad and the Consul keep watch.

### 1.7 Chapter 6, Chronos Keep [MED]

- The tram ends at **Chronos Keep**, a huge abandoned castle-like resort in the Bridle Range above the plain of the Tombs, used by the Shrike Church. BookRags calls it a homage to the Shrike. The pilgrims find it eerie but safe and decide to sleep there and go down to the Tombs in the morning. The Fandom page on Hyperion fauna puts black birds called **harbingers**, with obscenely human faces, near the Keep [MED-LOW].
- **The seventh pilgrim.** The Consul says there is no point telling his tale: with Masteen gone the pilgrimage is broken, since the group is no longer a prime number. The others argue about who might count as the seventh: Rachel, Lamia's unborn child, Duré (through his cruciform on Hoyt), or the Keats persona Lamia carries. They decide to go on regardless. They begin to suspect the Consul can call his ship to the valley.
- **The sky.** Space battle lights cascade overhead as the Ousters test the FORCE defences. **Hoyt spots a tall robed figure** striding with purpose across the flashing vermilion sands toward the Tombs. They take it to be Masteen; he will reach the Tombs hours before them. They plan to meet him there in the morning.
- **The Consul's Tale**, told in the Keep. Like Hoyt, the Consul says he can only tell his own story by first telling someone else's. BookRags says he switches on an old recorder or recording he has brought, and the group listens to his grandfather Merin Aspic's first-person account [MED-LOW]. His confession follows. The group's reaction is in §3.6.
- **After the confession.** The Consul sleeps, apparently at peace for the first time in years (Shmoop).

## 2. The lot-drawing [HIGH: official sample]

- **Who proposed telling the stories: Sol Weintraub.** He argues that as interstellar travellers carrying different amounts of time-debt, they are "islands of time". Each of them may hold a piece of a puzzle nobody has solved since humanity first landed on Hyperion, and he admits he enjoys mysteries. Het Masteen agrees, saying it may be wise to tell their stories before facing the Shrike. Lamia asks what would stop them lying. Silenus: nothing, that's the beauty of it.
- **Who proposed the vote: the Consul.** He thinks it might be a way to unmask the spy, then laughs at the idea of a spy that stupid. Kassad drily asks who decided they were a democracy and suggests electing a leader. Silenus refuses rudely, and everyone else shakes their head.
- **Masteen's condition:** all of them tell their stories or none do; they will abide by the majority.
- **The vote:**
  - Yes: Weintraub, Masteen, Silenus (who says he wouldn't trade this farce for a month in the orgasm baths of Shote) and the Consul, who surprises himself.
  - No: Hoyt (weakly) and Lamia ("stupid").
  - Kassad shrugs and abstains.
  - Result: 4 yes, 2 no, 1 abstention. Passed.
- **The lots.** Nobody volunteers to go first. **Martin Silenus** writes the numbers **1 to 7** on a slip of paper, tears it into strips and suggests they draw. Lamia calls it childish; Silenus answers that he is a childish fellow. He borrows the Consul's soft **gold tricorne** as the hat. The folded slips go round the table. **Sol Weintraub draws first and Silenus draws last.**
- **Results.** The Consul secretly unfolds his slip: **number 7**. He is relieved, hoping that events, the war or simple boredom will stop the tales before his turn comes. Hoyt holds up a clear **1**. The rest of the order is not stated at the draw, but it follows from who tells which tale:

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Father Lenar Hoyt | Priest's Tale (Yggdrasill) | HIGH (stated) |
| 2 | Col. Fedmahn Kassad | Soldier's Tale (Benares) | HIGH (by telling order) |
| 3 | Martin Silenus | Poet's Tale (Benares) | HIGH (BookRags: "drawn the next number") |
| 4 | Sol Weintraub | Scholar's Tale (windwagon) | HIGH (by telling order) |

The Consul hoped to dodge his turn by holding number seven. The irony is that he was always last, and Masteen's disappearance briefly gives him an excuse to skip it.

## 3. The six tales in detail

### 3.1 The Priest's Tale: "The Man Who Cried God" (teller: Father Lenar Hoyt) [HIGH: official sample, unless marked]

**Framing line.** Hoyt opens by saying that only a thin line sometimes separates orthodoxy from apostasy (paraphrase). The narrator says the Consul later dictated the tale into his comlog as one seamless account.

**Part A: Hoyt's introduction**
- **Hoyt** was born, raised and recently ordained on **Pacem**, the Catholic world with the New Vatican. As a boy he worshipped **Father Paul Duré**. Duré is a Jesuit, a follower of St. Teilhard, an archaeologist, ethnologist and eminent theologian, and could have been a bishop or even pope in another age.
- **Armaghast.** Duré led a Church-funded dig on the nearby world of Armaghast. He came back under a seal of secrecy, amid whispers of excommunication and even an Inquisition hearing (the first in four centuries). He had **faked the dig's results** to suggest a Christ-oriented alien culture about 600 light-years from Old Earth and some 3,000 years before humanity left Earth, hoping to revive the dying Church. Instead of facing excommunication he asked for exile to Hyperion.
- **The escort.** Hoyt was ordered to escort him to the Keats spaceport and return immediately on the same ship. That meant 20 months in fugue, a week of in-system travel at each end, and 8 years of time-debt. Hoyt obeyed without complaint.
- **The ship.** They travelled on the spinship **HS *Nadia Oleg***: a pock-marked metal tube with no artificial gravity off-drive, a few portholes and sex stimsims fed to passengers in hammocks and fugue couches. The passengers were off-world labourers, tourists, cult mystics and would-be Shrike suicides. The final glide from spinout to Hyperion takes 12 days in zero-g.
- **Duré's plan.** On board, Hoyt (who has a comlog implant) learns that Duré plans to travel to the southern continent **Aquila**, go inland via **Port Romance**, and set up an ethnological research station at **the Cleft** on the **Pinion Plateau**, to study the **Bikura**. The Bikura are thought to be a legend. The only report is from the explorer Mamet Spedling of the Shackleton Institute on Renaissance Minor, almost 150 years ago. Spedling described them as degenerate seedship survivors too dull to be worth describing. He fled the flame forests, losing four porters, all his records and his left arm.
- **Duré's reasons.** Hoyt argues for bigger mysteries: the Time Tombs, the Shrike north of the Bridle Range on Equus, and Hyperion's status as one of the **nine labyrinthine worlds**. Duré says those have hundreds of scholars, while the Bikura may vanish. He smokes a pipe in zero-g.
- **The parting.** On the dropship down, Duré murmurs that such sights give him an inkling of what it cost the Son of God to become the Son of Man. They part at **Keats Interstellar spaceport**. Hoyt is back on Pacem five weeks later, his own time, having lost 8 years. The bishop tells him Duré has not been heard from in the four years he has been on Hyperion.
- **The search.** The Consul confirms that his attaché Theo searched for years and found only early sightings in Port Romance. There are hundreds of radio-less plantations out there, many growing illegal drugs alongside fiberplastic. Hoyt volunteered to go back, was granted an audience with His Holiness, landed a month after the Consul's successor took office, and spent fewer than seven local months on Hyperion.

**Part B: Duré's journal** (dated by "Day N of my exile"). It begins with a line Hoyt reads out ("So begins my exile", quote).

1. **Day 1, Keats.** Duré cannot decide how to date his journal:
   - Pacem's monastery calendar: the 17th day of the month of Thomas, AD 2732.
   - Hegemony Standard: 12 October, 589 A.D.C.
   - Hyperion: the 23rd of **Lycius** (the last of seven 43-day months), either 426 "After Dropship Crash" or the 128th year of Sad King Billy's reign.

   He sees two of the three continents from the dropship: Equus and Aquila (Ursus is out of sight). Keats has hazy blue mountains to the north, orange and yellow trees on the foothills, a pale blue-green sky and a small but bright sun. Hyperion's colours look vivid from afar and dissolve up close, "like a pointillist's work" (paraphrase). The big sculpture of Sad King Billy disappoints him: crude, like a rough draft hacked from dark mountain stone, brooding over a patchwork city of half a million people. The city divides into **Jacktown** (a maze of slums and saloons) and the "Old City" (polished stone and deliberate sterility, only 400 years old). He writes to his boyhood friend **Monsignor Edouard** (they were boys together in **Villefranche-sur-Saône** and seminarians together): punished, but not yet repentant.
2. **Day 5, the ruined cathedral of Keats.** Empty for at least two standard centuries. It stands on the Hoolie bank where the Old City meets Jacktown's warehouses. The nave is open to the sky, one west tower unfinished and the other a skeleton, the chapter house half fallen into the river, the façade covered in grim apocalyptic carvings. Out of reflex Duré starts to say Mass at the bare altar, where the great ceramic cross lies shattered. A **blind old woman in black** is kneeling in the fourth pew. She leaves and vanishes through a side door to the river, but a lone **red votive candle** she lit is still burning. Duré turns sour on Keats: three Zen Gnostic centres, four Muslim High Mosques, saloons, brothels, the fiberplastic market and Shrike temples. He calls Hyperion "a poet's world without poetry" (quote, Duré's journal). He decides to take the weekly passenger airship south.
3. **Days 10–11, the airship.** A vast cargo dirigible. Duré camps by the rear cargo lock next to a plantation family's pigs and rooster. Three days along the ragged east coast of Equus, called "**the Mane**", then across to **Cat Island** and its "capital" **Felix** (about 5,000 people in shacks). Then 800 km along an island chain called the **Nine Tails**, and 700 km of open sea across the equator to Aquila's north-west coast, "**the Beak**". At dinner, retired professor **Heremis Denzel** (from a small planters' university near the town of **Endymion**) tells him the continents' official names are Creighton, Allensen and Lopez, after survey-service bureaucrats. From the promenade he sees a turquoise sea, a huge ray-like shadow keeping pace 300 m below, and a hummingbird-like flier with metre-wide wings. Duré confides his doubts to Edouard: about Teilhard's evolving Christ, about his sin at Armaghast, and his belief that the Church is dying.
4. **Day 28, Port Romance.** Eight days in town and three dead men seen: a bloated drowned body on the mudflats; a burned corpse pulled from a gutted shop; and a sandy-haired, slightly overweight stranger shot dead three metres from him, with no ID and six silver coins. Duré gives him last rites, then sits through the autopsy. The cynical little doctor weighs the heart in his hand and says a life is worth no more than this. The town is a tangle of yellow weirwood buildings on scaffolds and plank walkways over the mudflats at the mouth of the **Kans River**, where it meets **Toschahai Bay**; a dredge pounds all night. Duré cannot afford a company skimmer for his three crates of equipment, so he books a river passage.
5. **Day 41, up the Kans.** He travels on the ***Emporotic Girandole***, an ancient flat towboat with five ragged two-storey barges lashed around it. His bunk has a stained mattress and lizard-like insects on the walls. The jungle is a wall of chrome-yellow and butter-coloured light. Every afternoon brings a deafening deluge, then rising evening mist, glowbirds, and phosphorescent rot. Meteor showers fill the sky nightly, because Hyperion's orbit passes through debris. Orbital mirrors light the big plantations from above. **Old Kady**, a whittling, gap-toothed passenger, tells him there are no tesla trees down here, only up on the Pinion. They pass **Melton's Landing**.
6. **Day 60, Perecebo Plantation.** He arrives sick.
7. **Days 62–65, fever.** Black bile, delirium: the journal entry is a feverish jumble. A dark-haired woman with soft dark eyes nurses and bathes him. He recovers on Day 65.
8. **Day 66.** He shaves. His nurse is **Semfa**: from Port Romance, widowed three local months ago when her husband Mikel died in a harvest accident, and staying on doing odd jobs. The plantation administrator, **Orlandi**, is a quiet dark man with a slight lisp. He waives the medical bill and lends him a guide for the highlands: if they leave within ten days they might get through the flame forest to the Cleft before the tesla trees come fully active.
9. **Day 75.** He says goodbye to Semfa at the matrix paddies, blesses her and kisses her forehead. He leaves with the guide **Tuk** and two pack **brids**. Overseer Orlandi waves them off.
10. **Day 82.** After a week climbing through yellow rain forest they reach an overhang on the **Pinion Plateau** at almost 3,000 m. Clouds lie below; through gaps they see the Kans and the magenta fiberplastic fields near Perecebo.
11. **Day 83, the flame forest.**
    - **Vegetation.** The weirwood and fleshy-leaved **chalma** give way to evergreens and "everblues", mutant Murray pines and triaspens. Then the flame-forest species: islands of **prometheus** trees, **phoenix** shrubs, amber **flame-tongues/lambents**, and white fibrous forked **bestos (asbestos) plants**, which Tuk crudely compares to rotting giants' penises (Fandom quotes the line).
    - **The first tesla tree.** It is at least 100 m tall, nearly twice the height of the tallest prometheus. Near the crown is an onion-shaped **accumulator bulb**, with dozens of silvery, metallic **nimbus vines** on the wreathed branches above it. Duré thinks of a High Mosque on New Mecca hung with cheap tinsel.
    - **Gear and camp.** They put on stiff leathery **gamma-cloth** suits, **osmosis masks** and thick rubber-soled boots. Ozone smell. At night they hammer a ring of alloy **lightning-deflector stakes** around the tent, near an asbestos grove.
12. **Day 84, a tesla storm.** Three hours of apocalypse after midnight: hundreds of arcs, an exploding prometheus 30 m away. One terrified brid breaks out of the stake ring and is vaporised by half a dozen arcs. Two of the ten stakes fail. Tuk says they are made to last an hour or two before they melt.
13. **Day 87, the Cleft.** They come out of the smouldering north-east edge of the forest and sleep 18 hours. The surviving brid drops dead when unloaded. Duré follows a stream to a waterfall plunging almost 3,000 m into mist, rock and a river: **the Cleft**. Hyperion is tectonically dead, and the canyon formed like Mars's Mariner Valley, through freeze-thaw and underground rivers such as the Kans. Its walls are pink-tinted rock.
14. **Day 88. Tuk is murdered**, his throat cut while he slept outside the tent four metres from Duré. Duré blesses and buries him. The deflector stakes are stolen. Duré builds a shelter and starts mapping (Day 95).
15. **Day 96, the Bikura find him.**
    - **The village.** He spots about a dozen crude huts of piled chalma fronds, stones and sponge-moss on terraces and ledges down the overhang at the Cleft's edge.
    - **The Bikura.** He turns round to find at least 30 (then about 50) Bikura in a silent semicircle. They are short (none taller than his shoulder), **bald**, beardless, in rough-woven **dark robes from neck to toes**, gliding like phantoms. From a distance they look like a flock of shrunken Jesuits. Smooth faces, faintly **yellowish skin** (trace elements in chalma), all apparently aged 40 to 50. Round faces that look angelic at first glance, then placidly idiotic. Duré compares them to an old genetic condition; Shmoop quotes his line about "bald, retarded children" (quote, period-inappropriate, avoid in the game).
    - **First words.** The nearest asks, in debased seedship English that his comlog translates, whether he is "of the cruciform". He holds up the crucifix he wears, and they all kneel as one. They have no weapons. They lead him to the village to "sleep" (their two-hour afternoon rest).
16. **Day 97, "the Three Score and Ten".** (The German edition renders this as "Fünf Dutzend und Zehn".)
    - **Names.** Duré names them: **Alpha** (the first to approach; the closest to a leader and "male"-seeming), **Betty/Beta** (perhaps female, perhaps Alpha's mate) and **Del**.
    - **Tuk.** Alpha calmly admits they cut Tuk's throat with sharp stones and held him down until he stopped struggling. Tuk "died the true death"; Duré did not, because he is "of the cruciform" and follows the cross.
    - **Faith.** Names like Jesus, Mary and the Church mean nothing to them. The comlog says the seedship that crashed here 400 years ago carried **70 colonists, all Neo-Kerwin Marxists**.
    - **Daily life.** They weave on a primitive loom. At dusk the wind through the Cleft makes a vast **organ-pipe music**, and every evening all seventy vanish over the cliff edge and come back later, silently. Vines and roots hang down the cliff face.
17. **Days 103–104.** He moves his gear into the hut they keep for him and holo-scans everything, which they ignore. There are **no children**. They live on chalma tubers and roots, berries, and occasionally dead tree monkeys ("arboreals"). They have a strong **nudity taboo**. [Fandom adds the diet.]
18. **Day 105, the Basilica** (described in the Day 106 entry).
    - **The descent.** While the Bikura are out foraging, Duré climbs about 150 m down the braided vine ladders and bridges to a 5 m-wide ledge. He finds a path worn centimetres deep into solid rock, with steps sagging in the middle, used for millennia before the colonists arrived. The ledge runs north–south and faces the Cleft's western opening, so the setting sun (on the equinoxes, he guesses) shines straight in.
    - **The façade.** Carved **portals** with fine stone jambs and lintels are cut into the cliff, flanked by **stained-glass-like windows** at least 20 m tall. The material is harder than glass, transparent, and its colours swirl like oil on water.
    - **Inside.** A ten-metre vestibule, then a second wall of the same material with one metal-framed door. Beyond it, one gigantic hall hewn from solid rock, bigger than Pacem's full-scale replica of St Peter's Basilica. There is no ornament except a **5 m-square altar block** left standing in the centre, and on it a **cross 4 m tall and 3 m wide**, carved like early Old Earth crucifixes and **inlaid with diamonds, sapphires, bloodstones, lapis, queen's tears, onyx** and more, facing the window wall to catch the sunset.
    - **What he concludes.** He believes it is thousands, perhaps ten thousand, years old: older than Christ. He calls it the "Basilica" and sees it as proof of God and salvation for the Church.
    - **Afterwards.** He also notices steps leading even further down. He tries to leave at once, but smoke from the flame forest drives him back. The forest is fully active for about 128 days (three local months), then quiet for two. He says Mass at the cliff edge.
19. **Day 107, prisoner.**
    - **Seen.** **Del** sees him bathing bare-chested at the stream above the falls and runs off. The whole tribe seizes him, and **Beta** cuts his clothes off with a sharp stone. Alpha tells the big Bikura with a scarred palm, whom Duré calls **Zed**, that he is not of the cruciform.
    - **The argument.** **Gamma**, **Delta-Two** and Beta repeat the litany that whoever does not follow the cross must die the true death. Duré shouts that he has prayed at their altar. Alpha asks: can he not become of the cruciform?
    - **Locked up.** They lock him in his hut and take everything.
20. **Day 110.** Zed and **Theta-Primus** bring him out. The tribe has lit a fire, the first he has ever seen them make, and is **burning his comlog, discs, chips, scanner, notes and clothes**: all his evidence. Alpha tells him he will become of the cruciform.
21. **Day 112, initiation** (written two days later).
    - **The Basilica at sunset.** All seventy lead him down the vines to the Basilica. The wind organ roars and the low sunlight fills the hall with amber, gold and turquoise. The jewelled cross seems to soak up the light and glow back.
    - **The stairs.** With torches, they climb down narrow, worn, rain-slick **steps carved into the cliff, all the way to the canyon floor**, a sheer descent through the night. They reach the bottom before dawn. The Kans is at least 70 m wide and roaring.
    - **The labyrinth.** In the opposite cliff, and in theirs, are perfect **30 m-square openings**: entrances to **Hyperion's labyrinth**. Labyrinths exist on nine worlds, are at least three-quarters of a million years old, and are always found on Earth-like but tectonically dead worlds. Their tunnels are 30 m square and 10–30 km deep, cut by unknown builders with unknown technology, and nothing has ever been found in them; on Svoboda, remote probes have mapped 800,000 km of them.
    - **The cruciform cave.** About 100 m inside, where the tunnel's eroded mouth gives way to smooth floor, they put out the torches. A **pink glow** grows from hundreds of **cruciforms** studding the walls and ceiling. They range from millimetres to almost a metre across, are organic, warm, and look like soft coral.
    - **The Shrike.** The Bikura kneel with heads bowed as **the Shrike** enters and glides among them:
      - **Size and surface.** At least 3 m tall, its silver surface rippling like suspended quicksilver.
      - **Blades.** Curved metal blades jut from its forehead, its four wrists, oddly jointed elbows, knees, armoured back and torso.
      - **Arms.** Its **four long arms** spread like a pope blessing the faithful, the fingers clicking out like **chrome scalpels**.
      - **Eyes.** Large, red, multifaceted eyes with a blood-red glow burning inside the barbed skull.
      - **Movement.** Without moving it is suddenly less than a metre from Duré, fencing him in with blades. He sees his own white face reflected in its carapace. He feels something closer to exaltation than fear, waiting for its embrace. Then it is simply **gone**: no sound, no smell, no rush of air.
    - **The cruciform.** Alpha spreads his arms in a pathetic imitation and says "Cruciform". In a litany the tribe repeats, they pull a small (about 10 cm) cruciform off the wall; its glow dies. Alpha ties a short cord through the little knobs on its top arm and hangs it round Duré's neck. Its back is perfectly flat and smooth.
    - **The climb back.** He collapses on the climb and wakes in his hut, having lost a day; they must have carried him up 2.5 vertical km.
    - **Fused.** When he goes to bathe, **the cruciform will not come off**: it has fused to his chest, painlessly. The knife, beam weapon, scissors and razor are all gone. His medscanner shows internal fibres spreading through his body like roots and **nematodes**, from a node over the sternum to the basal ganglia and amygdalae on both sides of the brain. The tissue is **his own DNA**. "I am of the cruciform" (paraphrase).
22. **Day 116.** Now **leashed**: the cruciform will not let him go more than about 10 km from the Cleft. Walking into the flame forest brings heart-attack pain, which stops when he turns back. He finds the rusted, vine-covered wreck of the original **seedship's dropship** near the gorge.
23. **Day 117.** The scans show the Bikura are **sexless**, with no genitals at all, inside or out, and only a crude cloaca. Beta, Del and Theta let him scan them. He is still a man, for now.
24. **Day 133, Alpha's death.**
    - **The fall.** Alpha slips on wet boulders while they gather chalma tubers and falls 80 m to a ledge, dying instantly with his skull crushed. Duré gives last rites and hauls the body up.
    - **The Bikura stop him burying it.** Gamma forbids it: "He is of the cruciform." The cruciform on the corpse is **warm**. Seventy is now sixty-nine.
    - **In the Basilica.** They lower the body in a vine basket, lay it on the altar and chant the litany. Overnight Duré watches the corpse decompose at time-lapse speed, held together by a glowing mesh of fibres, then rebuilt. On the third day Alpha breathes again: whole, with a smooth doll's face fixed in a faint smile, and vacant.
25. **Day 140.** Duré tries to cut the cruciform out with a sharpened stone and keeps blacking out. It does not like pain.
26. **Day 158.** Alpha speaks a little. Scans show the organs of a teenage boy.
27. **Days 173–174, Will.** **Will** (the one with the broken finger) dies in a fall into a gorge and is eaten to the bone by red "fire ants". The Bikura take his cruciform and leave the skeleton. Duré thinks Will has died the true death. Then he finds that **Theta now carries two cruciforms**: when Theta dies, two will rise and the Seventy will be whole again.
28. **Day 195.** Duré asks why God allows this obscenity, why the Bikura are punished, and why he was chosen to share their fate.
29. **Day 214.** He concludes that the Bikura have achieved humanity's dream of immortality at the cost of their humanity and souls. He rediscovers blind faith and resolves to use **pain as his ally** against the parasite.
30. **Day 215.** He gets 14 km into the dormant forest before the pain drops him. He crawls back toward the Cleft in a daylong fight for his own body, and the Bikura carry him home. Further failures follow on Day 223.
31. **Day 257.** He turns 68 standard years old. He is building a **chapel** near the Cleft. Beta and four others stop him climbing down to the river.
32. **Day 280.** One local year on Hyperion: "purgatory, or hell?"
33. **Day 311.** While quarrying stone below the chapel ledge he finds the **deflector stakes** the Bikura threw over the edge the night they killed Tuk. Theta has broken a leg; the Bikura cut Theta's throat after four days so the cruciform could resurrect him rather than endure the pain. But the scanner had already shown the parasite's fibres **retreating under prolonged pain**. Duré has an idea.
34. **Day 438, the last entry.** The chapel is finished; he calls it his life's work. While the Bikura are at their daily worship below, he says Mass with chalma-flour bread and water for wine; it looks blood-red at sunset. His journals and medscan printouts go into a woven asbestos-fibre bag. His plan is to get deep enough into the flame forest. He says farewell to Edouard, hoping to see him in the next life, and that he has found peace. He prays the Act of Contrition. The final note: sunset light in the chapel window and the last wind-chorus.

**Part C: Hoyt's public ending.** Hoyt arrived ten weeks after he last saw Duré, by his own time; more than eight years had passed on Hyperion, seven since the last entry. Within four weeks he reached **Perecebo**. **Orlandi** and his new wife **Semfa** remembered Duré; unprecedented flame-forest activity had defeated their rescue attempts. Orlandi found two bush pilots, and they flew **two skimmers** up the Cleft itself to skirt most of the forest, losing "one skimmer and four people to tesla activity". They found the 70 Bikura, confirmation that Duré had died trying to cross the flame forest, and the asbestos bag with the journals. His remains were burned and decayed; the tesla discharges had destroyed body and cruciform, the **true death**. They buried him at Perecebo after a funeral Mass. "Over my strong objections" Orlandi destroyed the village and part of the cliff with **nuclear charges** brought from the plantation (used there for clearing jungle). The Basilica and the labyrinth entrance were apparently buried in the landslide. Hoyt was injured and recovered for months at the plantation. Only Orlandi, **Monsignor Edouard** and Edouard's superiors know of the journals, and the Church has said nothing publicly.

**Part D: the truth (Hoyt's confession to the Consul in his pod)**
- **The crash.** The skimmer was not lost to tesla activity. A storm forced down Hoyt's skimmer, with **Semfa**, a pilot and another man aboard, near the Cleft, while Orlandi searched upriver and had to wait out the weather.
- **The Bikura.** They came in the night and killed Semfa, the pilot and the other man, but spared Hoyt because of his crucifix. They told him of the way of the cross and of the **"Son of Flames"**.
- **Duré on the tree.** At dawn they led him about 3 km into the flame forest to a huge **tesla tree**, 80–100 m tall, quiescent but humming with charge, the ground covered in ash. The Bikura knelt at a distance. Duré had used a ladder to climb 3–4 m up the trunk and built a small asbestos platform for his feet. He snapped and sharpened the deflector stakes into spikes and drove the longest through his feet into the platform and tree. He drove one through his **left forearm between ulna and radius** (missing the arteries, as the Romans did) and hooked his **right hand** onto a spike sharpened at both ends. Then he kicked the asbestos ladder away.
- **Seven years.** Clothes, skin and outer flesh were long burned away, with nerves showing like grey and yellow roots, yet it was still recognisably Paul Duré. The current never stopped flowing through him. For seven years he lived, died and was forced back to life by the cruciform, feeding off whatever it could. But **the pain won**.
- **The release.** Hoyt climbed up on the fallen ladder and lifted off the asbestos bag. The **cruciform fell away** with long bloody roots. The "corpse" raised its head, lidless and lipless with eyes like boiled eggs, **smiled at him**, and **died truly** in his arms: for the ten-thousandth time, but for real this time.
- **Afterwards.** The Bikura took Hoyt back to the Cleft. Orlandi arrived the next day, maddened by Semfa's death, **lasered the village**, burned the Bikura as they stood like sheep, and then levelled the site with nukes. Hoyt **did not stop him. He laughed** (Shmoop quotes this).
- **The pain since.** Painkillers worked at first, but it has got worse every year, every day, even in fugue. He had to come back anyway.
- **Reveal.** Two cruciforms. One is Hoyt's own, given by the Bikura, on his chest, "like a big raw cross-shaped worm" (paraphrase). The other, Duré's, sits between his shoulder blades and stirs under the Consul's fingers. So Hoyt carries Duré's DNA and persona. [Fandom confirms the Bikura gave him both.] **Why Hoyt has come back:** to ask the Shrike to remove the cruciforms and end his suffering [MED, Fandom]. He survives on ultramorphine.

### 3.2 The Soldier's Tale: "The War Lovers" (teller: Colonel Fedmahn Kassad) [MED overall; items marked]

Told on the Benares after breakfast on the first morning upriver. It is narrated in third person about Kassad, as Kassad tells it.

1. **Agincourt** [HIGH, several sources]. The tale opens in the middle of the **Battle of Agincourt, 1415**, an English–French medieval battle. It is really a **combat simulation** in the **Olympus Command School's Historical Tactical Network (HTN)** on Mars. The school sits on **Olympus Mons**. Kassad is a young cadet, about 23 (Shmoop) [MED]. Simulations are dangerously immersive, and cadets have been pulled dead from the immersion crèches. Kassad loves the bloodlust of battle. He chases a French knight into the woods and loses the hand-to-hand fight. As he is about to "die", a **mysterious woman** intervenes and batters the Frenchman (Shmoop says with hammer blows) so Kassad can finish him with a dagger through the visor or face. She is the most beautiful woman he has ever seen. They make love at once on the battlefield, next to the dead man's cold armour. When he wakes she is gone.
2. **Searching for her** [MED]. She is not in the school records. Kassad meets her again in later sims, in many battles, always in combat followed by sex; he privately calls her "Mystery" [Shmoop, MED-LOW]. A fellow cadet tells him the HTN does not simulate but "dreams", and lets them dream with it (quote, Shmoop). Kassad keeps the affair secret. After a while she stops appearing.
3. **Background** [MED, Fandom]:
   - **Mars.** Born on Mars, in the poor and violent **South Tharsis Relocation Camps**, one of a minority who still call themselves **Palestinians**. Their ancestors were displaced after the "Nuclear Jihad of 2038" (Shmoop). By 16 he had killed a young man in gang life.
   - **The Brigade.** At 18 a Tharsis judge gave him a choice: a polar work camp, or the **John Carter Brigade**, volunteers helping FORCE against the revived **Glennon-Height rebellion**. He chose the Brigade and found he liked military discipline. It disbanded when he was 19, after Glennon-Height's cloned grandson died on Renaissance Vector.
   - **Lusus.** FORCE:ground rejected him. After a drunken binge he woke in the hives of **Lusus** with his comlog stolen and his farcaster access revoked, and did a year of heavy labour there, getting even more muscular under the high gravity.
   - **Maui-Covenant.** He shipped out to **Maui-Covenant** days before its Island War began (the "Siri's Rebellion" of the Consul's tale, see §3.6). He enlisted in a supply regiment as an assistant hydrofoil driver and was soon **Corporal Kassad**. He won two Distinguished Service Clusters, a Senate Commendation for valour in the **Equatorial Archipelago** campaign and two Purple Hearts, and was tapped for command school.
   - **Code and faith.** He lives by the **New Bushido** code, under which honour lies in combat between equals and civilians are spared. He was raised **Muslim** and believes the God of Islam would not condone the slaughter of innocents (Shmoop paraphrasing).
4. **Qom-Riyadh** [MED]. As a FORCE captain, Kassad is the ranking officer on the aging orbital defence picket **HS *Denieve*** (no nuclear weapons), the only Hegemony ship within two light-years. It is sent to deal with the **New Prophet**, leader of the New Order Shi'ites, who has started a holy war on the Muslim world **Qom-Riyadh**. The planet has two continents: Qom in the south and Riyadh in the north. Kassad goes down and, during a broadcast pronouncement to the whole planet, kills the New Prophet with a concentrated beam from orbit (Shmoop: a "satellite beam"). The uprising collapses. He faces a FORCE court-martial review on **Freeholm** and becomes famous. That night the woman visits him in a dream for the first time in years, but he wakes before she can tell him something.
5. **Bressia** [MED]. Kassad commands FORCE ground troops against an **Ouster invasion of Bressia**. It is a brutal guerrilla war: Fandom's plot summary speaks of a 3,000-ship Ouster fleet and 97 days of fighting, and Shmoop says Kassad called in a devastating **space strike** [MED-LOW on numbers]. He earns the name "**Butcher of South Bressia**". FORCE sees him as a hero; others call him a war criminal. A command conference is held on **HS *Brazil*** the night before the Ouster retreat. Two days after the Ousters withdraw, a **booby trap** badly wounds him. The woman visits him after the battle.
6. **The *Merrick*** [MED]. He is evacuated on the triple-hulled hospital spinship **HS *Merrick***. Ousters attack it in the **Hyperion system**. Kassad, recovering, arms himself and fights the Ouster boarders. Ousters are long-limbed, with prehensile toes and prosthetic tails useful in zero-g (Shmoop). He steals an Ouster boarding craft and **crash-lands on Hyperion**.
7. **Moneta** [MED]. He wakes with the woman bending over him: they are finally together in the real world. He is in or near the **City of Poets**, close to the Time Tombs (Fandom plot summary; Shmoop says "Mystery's room"). For the first time she tells him her name: **Moneta** (Shmoop and the French source both say she also gives "Mnemosyne") [MED]. She tells him an Ouster ship that followed him down has already been massacred by the Shrike.
8. **Living backward** [MED]. She takes him to the Time Tombs and explains their **anti-entropic fields**: the Tombs move backward through time, and so does she. Their meetings run in reverse for her: "your past, my future" (Shmoop quotes this) [HIGH, FunTrivia also says she moves backward].
9. **The Tree** [HIGH, multiple]. Kassad sees the Shrike's **Tree of Thorns**: a gigantic tree of steel/chrome with long thorns, with Ouster and human bodies impaled on it, still alive.
10. **The suit and the battle** [MED]:
    - **The suit.** Moneta covers him in a **silvery, mercury-like skinsuit**, taken from a tomb (Shmoop: from an orb). It covers them completely and gives tremendous strength and speed.
    - **The Shrike leads them.** Moneta calls it **the Lord of Pain** (Shmoop: "Pain Lord"). It leads them to an Ouster encampment or landing force.
    - **Frozen time.** Time has stopped. Moneta explains that the Shrike controls time's flow, and they move in "fast time". Time resumes and the three slaughter the Ousters effortlessly, Kassad killing with his bare hands. The Ousters retreat to space. Moneta says they will carry "the message" back to their kind.
    - **Honour lost.** Kassad slowly realises that this massacre without equal combat betrays his New Bushido honour.
11. **The transformation** [HIGH, multiple]. Afterward they make love as before. During the act Moneta changes: **steel thorns** grow from her skin, her teeth become **rows of steel blades**, and her lower body sprouts teeth or spikes (Shmoop). She floods him with **visions of a huge interstellar war**: fleets, burning worlds, billions dead. Kassad tears free, losing skin and blood, and expects to be killed. She returns to Moneta's form, but with **blood-red eyes and fang-like teeth**, and whispers his name (Shmoop).
12. **Aftermath** [MED]. Kassad passes out. Two days later Hyperion's **Self-Defense Force** finds him and treats him; he wakes days later and claims to remember nothing after the crash. He resigns his commission and fades from public life for about **16 years**, during which he was for a while active in anti-war movements (Shmoop paraphrasing).
13. **Discussion after the tale** [MED]. See §1.3. **Kassad's purpose:** to find the Shrike and Moneta and kill them, and so prevent the war he was shown and was perhaps meant to start.

### 3.3 The Poet's Tale: "Hyperion Cantos" (teller: Martin Silenus) [MED overall]

Told on the Benares the evening after Naiad. It is told in the first person and is very profane. Silenus starts with Hyperion's history, then jumps back to his own life when he realises the others are lost.

1. **Hyperion's second colonisation** [MED]. About two centuries before, **Sad King Billy** (King William XXIII of the Kingdom of **Windsor-in-Exile**, then on the Outback world **Asquith**) fled the rising **Glennon-Height** rebellion. At staggering expense he moved his kingdom to Hyperion with seedships (Wikipedia: five) and thousands of artists (BookRags: more than 2,000), plus android labour. They founded the **City of Poets** near the Time Tombs, north of the Bridle Range, as a "new Renaissance" for creativity. They also founded Keats, Endymion and other cities named for poets. They had no defence against the Shrike, whom Silenus calls Hyperion's **Grendel** (BookRags).
2. **Old Earth** [MED]. Silenus was born on **Old Earth** into one of its wealthiest families, which he makes no apology for, in the planet's last years after the **Big Mistake**. In that disaster a runaway black hole made in a laboratory by the **Kiev Team** fell into the Earth's core and slowly ate it, in decade-spanning convulsions (the "Bad Times"). His mother lived in luxury, ran up debts and spent hours a day in stimsims. He was educated "classically", without implants, by a tutor called **Balthazar** (Shmoop). He wrote bad poetry and lived wildly.
3. **The journey and the stroke** [MED]. Before Earth died, his mother sent him off on a **slow, slower-than-light ship** in cheap cryogenic fugue. The plan was that interest would accumulate on the family investments during the decades of travel (Shmoop: 129 years) and pay off the debts. She died with Old Earth. On arrival at **Heaven's Gate** he was **penniless**, because Earth's accounts were frozen or seized, and **brain-damaged**: the cheap fugue had caused a **stroke**. His vocabulary was reduced to **nine words**, almost all obscenities, and Shmoop says the mildest were childish toilet words.
4. **Heaven's Gate** [MED]. It orbits Vega Prime: originally boiling mud, pestilent swamps and unbreathable air, with terraforming under way. Silenus is put to slave-like labour digging canals and mud. As his brain heals his words return, and he decides that to be a true poet is to become God (paraphrasing Shmoop). He begins his epic, the ***Cantos***.
5. **Publication** [MED]. A thug named **Unk** beats him nearly to death while he is carrying the manuscript. **Helenda**, who reads it while he recovers (Fandom says the manager's wife), gets it published. His editor **Tyrena Wingreen-Feif** at **Transline Publishing** retitles it ***The Dying Earth***. She cuts the philosophy, the passages about his mother and the homages to earlier poets, keeping only the sentimental idyll of Earth's last days. It sells about **2.5 billion copies** (Shmoop character page; the chapter summary says "over a billion"), and Silenus becomes immensely rich. Helenda divorces her husband and marries him.
6. **Fame** [MED]. He buys a farcaster house with **38 rooms on 36 worlds** (paraphrasing Shmoop). He marries three times, drinks, and tries dozens of religions. He pushes for the unedited *Cantos*. Tyrena calls it a masterpiece that will not sell, and it sells only about **23,638 copies** (Shmoop). Under contract he grinds out *Dying Earth* sequels for years: Shmoop says eight weak sequels over six years, Fandom says up to the tenth. He calls hack writing easy. When he balks, Tyrena threatens to put him in the Gothic-romance factory under a silly pen name (Shmoop quotes the joke).
7. **To King Billy** [MED]. Disgusted, he liquidates and flees to **Sad King Billy**, a patron who loved the *Cantos* and bought thousands of copies to hand out. Billy brings him to Hyperion, though Silenus at first objects.
8. **Blocked in the City of Poets** [MED]. In the beautiful new city, built largely by androids, Silenus cannot write a line. He has a body sculptor turn him into a **satyr** (hairy flanks, goat legs, hooves, and a matching genital alteration) and spends years in debauchery, which he calls both wonderful and hell. He decides to kill himself, and then **the Shrike appears**.
9. **The killings** [MED]. People in the city start disappearing or being found mutilated. At first a serial killer is suspected; then it is clearly the Shrike. Silenus's verse suddenly flows with every killing. He comes to believe his poem summoned the Shrike and that the poem could not exist without it as his muse. He jokes callously about bathing in the victims' blood as a literary aphrodisiac (quoted by Shmoop, crude; do not use verbatim). **Billy evacuates** the city to Keats/Jacktown. About 200 people stay, Silenus among them. Over the following years (Shmoop: 12) the rest die or flee, until **Silenus is alone**. He has his satyr features removed. Then the killings stop, and so does the poem.
10. **Billy's death** [MED; details conflict, see §7]. One night Billy comes back. He tells Silenus the Cantos cannot be finished without more killings. Silenus attacks him and is knocked out with a **neural stunner**. He wakes in a **courtyard**, where Billy, having concluded that the manuscript is what calls the Shrike, is **burning the manuscript** with kerosene. **The Shrike appears and seizes Billy** in a spiked embrace. In Shmoop's version Silenus burns the rest of the manuscript himself, and the Shrike **vanishes, taking the impaled Billy with it**. Silenus effectively chose his poem over his friend.
11. **Coda** [MED]. Silenus spent a year rewriting the burned pages but could not finish; his muse had fled. Since then he has lived about two more centuries through **Poulsen treatments**, which turn the skin blue, and long cryogenic sleeps. He has come back so the poem can be finished "in the end will be the Word" (paraphrase of Shmoop's quote). Fandom adds a wish to atone for leaving Billy to the Shrike.

### 3.4 The Scholar's Tale: "The River Lethe's Taste Is Bitter" (teller: Sol Weintraub) [MED overall]

Told in third person on the windwagon after dinner.

1. **Barnard's World** [MED-HIGH]. Barnard's Star is a red dwarf about 6 light-years from Old Earth, and the world was an early colony. It is very **flat**: endless fields, with long, spectacular blood-red sunrises and sunsets. A quiet backwater. **Sol** teaches history, classics and **ethical evolution** at **Nightenhelser College** in the small town of **Crawford** [MED]. He is Jewish by heritage and not observant. He believes Israel and the Promised Land died with Earth, and when asked if he believes in God, he says he is waiting to (Shmoop).
2. **Family** [MED]. He met **Sarai** at university, and they live a happy small-town life. Late in life they have one daughter, **Rachel**: bright, warm and "cute without being precious" (paraphrasing Shmoop). As she grows up she and Sol argue about philosophy and religion.
3. **Rachel's work** [MED]. Rachel studies archaeology: classical and alien, pre-Hegira artifacts. Fandom places her graduate work at **Reichs University on Freeholm** under **Dr. Melio Arundez**. She goes to Hyperion on Arundez's team at the **Time Tombs** and becomes his lover. She studies the "time tides" and focuses on the **Sphinx**, the tomb least affected by them. The time-debt between Barnard's World and Hyperion means her ten days are months for her parents.
4. **The dream** [HIGH, multiple]. Around this time Sol starts having a vivid recurring dream. In it he walks through a vast space toward two red, glowing ovals (Shmoop), and a voice orders him to take Rachel, his only daughter whom he loves, to Hyperion and offer her as a **burnt offering** there: a deliberate echo of **God's command to Abraham** about Isaac. At first he thinks it absurd.
5. **The accident** [MED]. One night, working alone (or late) in the **Sphinx**, Rachel's equipment fails and the anti-entropic field surges. Rooms appear inside the structure, some larger than the building itself, and the power goes out. Fandom says she encounters the Shrike before losing consciousness. She is found **comatose** and taken for treatment; Shmoop says to a medical facility on **Renaissance Vector**.
6. **Merlin's Sickness** [HIGH, multiple]. When she wakes, doctors find she is **aging backward**: one day younger each day. Each time she sleeps she **loses a day of memory**. She wakes believing it is the day she entered the Sphinx, and every day she slips further back. The condition is called **"Merlin's sickness"**, after the wizard who lived backward. There is no cure. Experiments fail, and lab animals exposed to similar fields die. Each morning she plays herself a recorded explanation. The media dub her the "Virgin Victim of the Time Tombs' Curse" (quote, Shmoop).
7. **Living backward** [MED]. Rachel comes home to Barnard's World. Eventually the daily shock is too much, and she asks her parents to just pretend everything is normal. They agree: Sol and Sarai dress, furnish the house and behave as they did when she was 16, then 12, then 10 and younger. She returns to school at the levels she is regressing through, plays with friends at 7, loses reading at 5, then speech, then her hair, and gains baby fat. Fandom says Sol later moved to **Hebron** to escape the media circus [LOW-MED]. Melio Arundez comes to see them [MED-LOW].
8. **The book** [HIGH, multiple]. The dream keeps returning. Sol writes a book on the Abraham story. Paraphrasing his conclusion: any allegiance to a deity, idea or universal principle that puts obedience above decent behaviour toward an innocent person is evil. In the dream he refuses. Fandom's plot summary has the voice telling him he has had his chance and knows where to find it if he changes his mind [MED-LOW].
9. **Sarai** [HIGH, multiple]. When Rachel is about to forget how to read, Sarai confesses she has been having **the same dream**. She wants to take Rachel to Hyperion, and to offer **themselves** instead of Rachel. Sol refuses. Seeing how worn she is, he urges her to visit her sister. On that trip Sarai is killed in a **vehicle (skimmer) accident**.
10. **Pilgrimage** [MED]. Rachel's "birthday", when she will reach zero, is approaching. Sol, now alone, decides to take her to Hyperion to seek a cure from the Shrike, or at least to confront the voice. Shmoop claims Sol "turns to the cult of the Shrike". In practice the Shrike Church selects him, and he is not shown as a member [see §7]. The tale ends as Sol boards the treeship with infant Rachel, a few weeks old (Shmoop: seven weeks), who smiles at him: a moment that could be her last smile or her first.
11. **At the start of the pilgrimage** [HIGH, sample]. Sol says he is 68. Rachel is a few weeks old, carried in a sling. At the Yggdrasill dinner the narration calls her "the one-week-old baby" (German sample).

### 3.5 The Detective's Tale: "The Long Good-Bye" (teller: Brawne Lamia) [MED overall]

Told in the first person in hard-boiled noir style (the title nods to Chandler), in the tramcar after dinner.

1. **The client** [MED]. Brawne is a private investigator with a cheap, sleazy office in a hive city on **Lusus**: 1.3 g, with people living in vast stacked residential and industrial "**hives**". On a dark, drizzly day a strikingly beautiful man walks in. Shmoop quotes her crude reaction. He is **Johnny**, a **cybrid**: a human body grown from human genetic stock whose consciousness lives in the **TechnoCore**'s datumplane. He wants her to **investigate his own murder**. Someone killed his body, which disconnected him from the Core for about a minute and cost him the memory of the previous few days (Fandom says five).
2. **Who Johnny is** [HIGH, multiple]. He is the product of a Core **Personality Retrieval Project**: an AI rebuilt around the personality of the 19th-century poet **John Keats**. Brawne is named for Keats's love Fanny Brawne and for his poem "Lamia". Johnny remembers what his designers thought Keats felt. He believes Hyperion is "the key mystery of our age" (quote via Shmoop) and that he was born, died and was reborn to explore it.
3. **Madhya** [MED]. They farcast to **Madhya** (pronounced roughly "Mud-yeh"), an Outback colony of Parvati near Hyperion, not yet officially on the Web, with a sulphurous stink and one oversized moon. It has a farcaster in an empty mining area, where his body was found. Farcaster records suggest someone was with him. Fandom says the weapon was a virus ("AIDS II"). The attack on his body makes no sense, since an attack in datumplane would have been more effective.
4. **TC2 and BB** [MED]. They go to **Tau Ceti Center**, the Hegemony capital world. Brawne visits **BB (Ernest) Surbringer**, a college friend and lifelong "cyberpuke" hacker with a cortical shunt since age 12. He works for Hegemony **Flow Control Records and Statistics** and lies on a couch in a windowless TC2 worker block with **microleads plugged into his skull**. He explains AIs and cybrids, and says cybrid persona templates eventually decay.
5. **The bar and the second attack** [MED]. A source in a bar tells her Johnny had been seen at a table with a **Templar** and another man. Johnny later calls her in a panic, and she finds him **bloodied** at his residence after a second attack. She takes him to stay at her home for protection.
6. **The chase** [MED]. They spot the attacker. A breakneck chase follows through farcaster after farcaster across several worlds; she swallows a **melanin pill** to darken her skin for cover (Shmoop). She enjoys it. When she catches the man and fights him, he **self-destructs**: he was a cybrid too. With the authorities after her, Johnny pulls her through a farcaster to safety.
7. **Old Earth replica** [MED]. He brings her to a **replica of Old Earth** in the **Hercules Cluster**, which the Core secretly built for its own reasons. They spend the night in the replica of **Keats's house**, in Rome by the **Spanish Steps** [MED-LOW], and become lovers.
8. **The Shrike Temple** [MED]. Five attackers strike at dawn. Brawne fights them off and interrogates one, who says they are from the **Shrike Temple on Lusus** before he passes out. They go to the Temple, whose clergy wear blood red and black. The **Bishop** tells them that Keats's persona is closely tied to Hyperion. He also says that **eight days earlier Johnny himself volunteered for the Shrike Pilgrimage**, which explains the meeting with a Templar, whose treeship would carry the pilgrims. To reach Hyperion, which has no farcaster and no Core datasphere link, Johnny would have to **invest his whole consciousness in his cybrid body** and become effectively human, which the Core forbids.
9. **Gladstone** [MED]. They go to TC2 and meet CEO **Meina Gladstone**, a friend and protégée of Brawne's late father. She is evasive: Hyperion is a mystery to the Core because of the Tombs.
10. **The datumplane run** [MED]. BB, Brawne and Johnny break into the **Core's datumplane**. Johnny sacrifices much of himself to open the way, and Brawne and BB get the data. Brawne describes the AIs' wrath as making them "insects under elephants' feet" (paraphrase of Shmoop's quote). **BB is killed**: the Core's defences destroy his brain. Johnny obtains his persona and invests it fully in his cybrid. A **Schrön loop**, the most advanced data storage in the Hegemony, is set up as storage (Fandom: BB downloaded Keats's persona to it).
11. **What they learn** [MED]. The Core has three factions:
    - **Stables**: want AI and human coexistence.
    - **Volatiles**: see humanity as obsolete and a threat, and want to eliminate it.
    - **Ultimates**: care only for building the **Ultimate Intelligence (UI)**, an AI god.

    The Core predicts human events with near-certainty (Shmoop: 98.9995%, up to two centuries), but **Hyperion** is the one variable it cannot predict. They also learn two competing theories of the Shrike: either a Core faction from a future where AIs rule sent it back as a retroactive first strike on humanity, or future humans sent it back to destroy the Core. Either way, a man-versus-AI war lies ahead.
12. **Johnny's death** [HIGH, multiple]. Unable to farcast safely, they head for the Shrike Temple on Lusus to wait for passage. **Core agents ambush them near the Temple and Johnny is killed**. Before dying he **transfers his memories and persona into the Schrön loop implanted at the base of Brawne's skull/neck**. His last word is reportedly "Fanny" (quote, Shmoop).
13. **Her father** [MED]. Brawne's father, **Senator Byron Lamia**, Gladstone's friend and mentor, was found dead about twenty years earlier and declared a suicide. Brawne never believed it. He and Gladstone had been working on a bill to bring Hyperion into the Protectorate. She suspects the Core killed him. Her mother withdrew into seclusion, partly at the family's estate on **Freeholm**.
14. **The reveal** [HIGH]. Brawne is **"pregnant twice"** (paraphrase of a quote): with Johnny's child, since his body was human enough to father one, and with the Schrön-loop memory of what he was. The Shrike Church gave her sanctuary and a place on the pilgrimage (Miscellany of Fiction). Her goal: to take Johnny, now in the loop, to Hyperion, as he wanted.

### 3.6 The Consul's Tale: "Remembering Siri", then the confession [MED overall]

Told at Chronos Keep. The first part is the first-person story of **Merin Aspic**, which the listeners only realise is the Consul's grandfather at the end. It cuts between "now" (Merin climbing to Siri's tomb) and flashbacks to his reunions with Siri.

1. **Opening, the tomb** [MED]. On a perfect day that Merin hates for being perfect (paraphrasing Shmoop's quote), he climbs a hill to **Siri's tomb** on an island of the **Equatorial Archipelago** of **Maui-Covenant**. It is the **day the planet's farcaster is to be activated**. He leaves his son and grandchildren below. He remembers first coming to this hilltop with Siri.
2. **The ship and time-debt** [HIGH, multiple]. Merin is a young shipman on the Hegemony spinship ***HS Los Angeles***, part of a **farcaster construction** project for Maui-Covenant. Every leave he gets passes in a few months of his own time but years on the planet. Over the whole story he ages about five years while Siri lives about 65 (Shmoop: he has known her 5 years, she has known him 65). He sees her only about **six or seven times** in her life.
3. **Maui-Covenant** [HIGH]. An ocean world whose people live on **motile isles**: living floating islands steered by sail-like leaves and rudder-like vines, which migrate to the equator every summer. They are herded by **dolphins** saved from Old Earth's last days. The colonists are Covenant settlers who want to protect their world and are wary of joining the Hegemony.
4. **First meeting** [MED]. Merin and his fun-loving friend **Mike Osho** (Shmoop casts him as Mercutio to Merin's Romeo) fly a **Hawking mat** to a festival. Fandom says Mike bought the old flying carpet in a market on **Garden** for 30 marks, and it was later passed to Merin. Some summaries name the festival "Firstsite" [LOW]. Merin meets **Siri**, a local girl (16 by most accounts [LOW]), and they become lovers at once. She teaches him about her world's life and ecology. They swim with the dolphins and talk to them through **translator discs or medallions**. The French source says the dolphins' language is simple but surprisingly effective.
5. **Mike's death** [MED]. Siri's cousin **Bertol**, an anti-Hegemony separatist, confronts and insults the Hegemony spacers. In the fight that follows **Mike Osho is killed by a sword**; Shmoop quotes his comic, profane dying complaint about "cutlery". Fandom's plot summary says Merin killed Bertol with an industrial laser pen and fled with Siri [LOW-MED]. Mike's death is the start of Merin's change of loyalty.
6. **Reunions** [MED]. On later visits Siri is older each time and has borne his children (sons **Alon** and **Donel**; see §7). At the **sixth reunion** Merin is about 22–23 and Siri has just turned **70**. She has become wealthy and a respected leader, and has been faithful to him through decades apart. Shmoop quotes Merin pitying her: at 70 she has never been offworld, never stepped through a farcaster, had no formal schooling, and flown nothing but an ancient family Vikken skimmer. She asks what the farcaster will mean. Merin explains:
   - Billions of WorldWeb tourists will be able to step straight onto Maui-Covenant.
   - Its oil will be drilled for the Web's plastics.
   - The isles and the dolphins will be destroyed.
   - Its culture will be absorbed into the Hegemony.

   Destroying the farcaster would only delay the Hegemony, about eleven years before FORCE could return, and resistance would be crushed.
7. **The tomb, again** [MED]. The tomb is empty: Siri was **cremated**. **Donel**, the son who guided him up, says she left something for Merin. Inside a box are her **comlog** with years of diary messages recording her devotion to him, and the **Hawking mat**. Sources disagree on whether there is also a weapon, a communications device or a bomb; see §7.
8. **The farcaster** [MED]. After hearing her messages, Merin acts. As the new **orbital farcaster** is switched on, it flares and **explodes**: he has sabotaged it, by a planted bomb or a coded trigger. He and the hawking mat head off to join the separatists. He says he will tell the rebels about Siri and "sing to them of Siri" (paraphrasing Shmoop's quote).
9. **The Consul steps out of the story** [HIGH, multiple]. He reveals that **Merin Aspic was his grandfather**:
   - **Siri's Rebellion** lasted about two years. Merin died in it.
   - FORCE won. The motile isles were wrecked and **the dolphins were wiped out**. Maui-Covenant was industrialised and opened to tourists.
   - The Consul's father, **Donel**, a Hegemony loyalist, became Maui-Covenant's first Home Rule Councillor and later a **Senator**. The Consul saw this as weakness: embracing the Hegemony instead of fighting it.
   - Fandom says the Consul's sister **Lira** died fighting with the rebels.
   - The Consul closes by echoing his grandfather: he remembers Siri. He also quotes Mercutio: "A plague on both your houses" (quote, Shakespeare, via Shmoop).
10. **The Consul's career** [MED, Fandom + Shmoop]:
    - After schooling on TC2 he became a liaison to oil corporations on Maui-Covenant, where he met his wife **Gresha**. He then joined the diplomatic corps and requested postings outside the Web.
    - As Under Consul on **Whirl, Garden and Hebron** he helped colonies destroy native life as the price of joining the Protectorate; Hebron's empathic natives, the **Seneschai Aluit**, are a notable example. The Hegemony routinely eliminates indigenous species it judges a threat.
    - After Hebron he broke down, with alcohol and the drug **Flashback**. The sample confirms his alcoholism treatment at **St. Francis Hospital on Hebron** after his first disastrous mission, nearly four decades ago.
    - As full Consul on **Bressia**, he watched the Hegemony deliberately provoke the **Ouster invasion** as a test of Ouster strength, through lend-lease torchships and impact probes for the militaristic Bressians. He stayed on through a "miscalculation". **His wife Gresha and son Alon were killed** in the fighting. This is the dead child mentioned in Chapter 1 [HIGH link].
11. **Working for Gladstone** [MED]. Then-Senator **Meina Gladstone** recruited him for a secret plan: to **provoke the Ousters into attacking Hyperion**. The aim was to destroy them as a threat and to bring Hyperion into the Web, which would shift the balance of power with the Core in humanity's favour. The Hegemony gave him a private ship (the ebony ship with the antique **Steinway**). He travelled alone for months until the Ousters captured him, as planned.
12. **Double agent** [MED]. Among the Ousters he became a true double agent:
    - He told them the Hegemony's plans.
    - He found them sophisticated, not barbarians. They adapt themselves to space instead of terraforming worlds into copies of Old Earth.
    - They showed him proof that **Old Earth's destruction was not an accident**: the "Big Mistake" was deliberately arranged by elements of the **TechnoCore** and the fledgling Hegemony.
    - They believe the Shrike is a weapon or tool from humanity's future, meant to save them from the Core.
    - They promised him a **device that would open the Time Tombs**.
13. **Back to Gladstone** [MED]. He told Gladstone part of the truth: the Ousters knew a battle at Hyperion would be a trap but would come anyway, and had asked him to be their agent. He did not mention the device. He declined promotion to ambassador and took the post of **Consul on Hyperion**. He served **11 years**, mostly leaving the governing to his aide **Theo Lane** and drinking at **Cicero's** while he waited.
14. **The betrayal of both sides** [MED-HIGH]. When the Ousters were ready, he took leave and met them in the system's Oort cloud. He brought an **Ouster agent and three technicians** to the **Time Tombs** north of the Bridle Range. While they worked, he learned the Ousters were divided over whether and how to invade, and were hesitating with the Hegemony fleet so close. **He killed all four** (Shmoop: "shoots the scouts") and **activated the device himself**, opening the Tombs and freeing the Shrike. He told the Ousters it was an accident caused by the Shrike. He told Gladstone the mission had succeeded but he needed solitude. Then he left for the empty swamp world of the Prologue. His motive: to destroy the Hegemony and the Core that killed Old Earth, Maui-Covenant, the dolphins and his family. Shmoop quotes him standing proud with fist raised against the sky.
15. **The comlog** [MED]. He admits he has been in contact with **his ship and the Ousters** the whole time through **Siri's old comlog**, which the Ousters modified. This bears on the others' suspicion that he can summon his ship.
16. **Reaction** [MED]:
    - The pilgrims are stunned: **the Consul is the spy** Gladstone warned of.
    - Kassad asks whether he attacked Het Masteen. The Consul denies it, and adds that Masteen's reaction to the Yggdrasill's destruction was far too calm: something is not as it seems.
    - The group argues over whether to kill him as a traitor. Shmoop says Silenus wanted to execute him.
    - **Sol Weintraub** argues that Gladstone and the Core must have expected the Consul to betray them, since both had betrayed his family; he was used and is no freer than any of them.
    - They decide to go on together as their best hope. The Consul falls asleep, at peace.

## 4. Character sheet (art direction)

The descriptions come from the novel where possible. **[S]** marks details verified in the official sample text; the rest follow the confidence tags.

### The seven pilgrims

**The Consul** (never named; teller 7). [S]
- **Appearance:** past middle age, tanned but oddly pale under **sad eyes**. Formal travel dress: loose black trousers, polished ship boots, a **white silk blouse** that balloons at waist and elbows, a **topaz collar clasp**, a black demi-coat with **Hegemony scarlet on the epaulettes**, and a **soft gold tricorne hat**, which becomes the lot-drawing hat.
- **Habits:** drinks scotch and whiskey. Plays a pre-Hegira Steinway (Rachmaninoff) and listens to Wagner in storms. Hunts big game.
- **Background:** homeworld **Maui-Covenant**. Former alcoholic (Hebron). Once a father, to Alon, now dead. Calm diplomat, quietly the voice of reason. Comlog: Siri's old, Ouster-modified one [MED].

**Father Lenar Hoyt** ("the Priest"; teller 1). [S]
- **Appearance:** Catholic priest in a black cassock and Roman collar. Early thirties but looks prematurely aged and long ill. A thin face with cheekbones pressing against sallow, pasty skin. Large eyes sunk in deep hollows. Thin lips pulled down by a permanent muscle twitch, too downturned even to read as cynical. A hairline "ravaged" as if by radiation. Underneath, traces of the round-faced, fair, soft-mouthed boy he was.
- **Body:** a **cruciform** on his chest and another between his shoulder blades. Hollow and fragile; the Consul carries him easily.
- **Background:** homeworld **Pacem**. Lives on **ultramorphine** injections.
- **Props:** two scorched notebooks (Duré's journals) and a crucifix on a chain. In the Epilogue he plays a **balalaika** at dawn (BookRags) [MED].

**Colonel Fedmahn Kassad** ("the Soldier"; teller 2). [S]
- **Appearance:** very tall, almost eye-level with the 2-metre Masteen. Wears **FORCE black** with no insignia or rank badges, which oddly mirrors Hoyt's black. Tanned, extremely fit and whip-lean, with cords of muscle at shoulders, forearms and neck. **Small, dark eyes**, watchful like camera lenses. A face all edges, planes and facets, as if carved from old stone, with a **thin strip of beard along the jawline** "like blood on a knife blade" (paraphrase). Moves slowly and powerfully, like an Earth-bred jaguar. Quiet voice; his silences draw attention.
- **Background:** homeworld **Mars** (Tharsis), Palestinian heritage. Known as the "Butcher of South Bressia".
- **Props:** brings weapons. Gives the Consul a **deathwand** on the windwagon. Has binoculars at Keats [S].

**Martin Silenus** ("the Poet"; teller 3). [S]
- **Appearance:** **short and visibly out of shape**. A face as mobile and expressive as an Earth primate's. **Ruddy/red cheeks, a wide mouth, bushy brows, pointed ears**, and hands in constant motion with **very long fingers**, like a concert pianist's or a strangler's. **Silver hair cut in uneven bangs/curls**. Looks late fifties, but **blue tinges at the neck and palms** betray many **Poulsen treatments**; the Consul guesses 90–150 standard years old, and he is in fact far older. Loud, profane, booming voice; "almost amiably demonic". Drinks heavily, two bottles of wine at dinner.
- **Costume:** at Keats, a **long purple cape and a red beret** slouched to the right shoulder.
- **Background:** born on **Old Earth**.
- **Props:** his manuscript (the unfinished *Cantos*). Formerly body-sculpted into a satyr; that has since been reversed.

### The Shrike [HIGH: official sample + Fandom + Wikipedia]
- **Size and shape:** roughly humanoid but in no way human; **about 3 m tall**.
- **Surface:** **chrome/silver steel** that seems to ripple and shift like **suspended quicksilver** even when still. It looks part mechanical, part organic.
- **Four arms:** two pairs, the lower slightly shorter; Fandom adds this. They are long and strangely jointed. The fingers snick out like **chrome scalpels**, and there are long curved **toe blades**.
- **Blades and thorns** everywhere: a **curved blade on the forehead** and another higher on the skull, blades at the **four wrists, elbows and knees**, an armoured back and torso, a large curved **thorn on the chest**, and rosettes of thorns at the joints. A "barbed skull".
- **Eyes:** large, **multifaceted red eyes** with a fierce **blood-red glow** burning inside, "where God meant eyes to be" (quote via Fandom). The Consul remembers a "hundred-faceted ruby-and-blood" stare.
- **Mouth:** rows of steel teeth.
- **Behaviour:** does not walk so much as **stop being "there" and be "here"**, because it moves in or controls time. Appears and vanishes silently, with no displacement of air. Communicates only through death. Kills or impales victims on the **Tree of Thorns**. The Shrike Church calls it the **Avatar** and **Lord of Pain**.

## 6. The ending (Epilogue) [MED, by BookRags + Shmoop + Fandom (page-cited)]

1. **Dawn at Chronos Keep.** The Consul wakes to **Father Hoyt playing a balalaika**. One by one the others gather. They are early, but decide to set off for the Tombs at once.
2. **Luggage.** They **leave nearly all their luggage behind**, taking only what each wants to have when facing the Shrike, since they will need nothing else either way. Expect the Möbius cube, Kassad's weapons, Silenus's manuscript, Sol with Rachel and Lamia's weapon to come along [LOW on the exact items].
3. **The descent.**
   - **Brawne Lamia leads.** They go down from the Keep (Shmoop: **661 steps**).
   - They cross **the moor** below the Keep, then the **low hills and dunes**, until they are level with the ruined **City of Poets**, and then continue down **into the valley of the Time Tombs** (Fandom, citing 1995 Bantam p. 479).
   - The **Sphinx and the Jade Tomb** come into view first (p. 480).
   - **The Tombs are glowing**: an unusual light that Sol notes was never reported during Rachel's research there.
4. **The song.** Nobody speaks. Then **Sol Weintraub** begins to hum, then sing, to Rachel a pre-Hegira song from an ancient flat film about going to see a wizard in Oz: **"We're Off to See the Wizard"**. The others, puzzled at first, join in. They **link arms** and walk side by side down into the valley singing, toward the Shrike, which they half-jokingly cast as "the wizard".
5. **End of the novel.** The book stops there, mid-journey, before they reach the Tombs. Nothing about what happens in the valley belongs in the game.

## 8. Sequel-only facts: do NOT use in a *Hyperion*-only game

These come from *The Fall of Hyperion* or later books and often appear on fan wikis. They must not leak into dialogue, item text or art:

- Moneta's true identity, and anything about her future.
- What the Shrike, the Tree of Pain or the Shrike Palace "really" are.
- The fates of any pilgrim after the valley: Hoyt, Kassad, Silenus, Masteen, Lamia, Sol and Rachel, the Consul.
- The second Keats cybrid ("Joseph Severn") and the AI Ummon.
- Who Brawne's child will be.
- What the Ousters' "invasion" really is, the Fall of the farcasters, and Gladstone's later actions.
- The Pax, the Church's later use of the cruciform, and Hoyt or Duré as popes.
- The Sphinx as a portal, and the erg's later role.

In *Hyperion* these are all open mysteries. Keep them that way.
