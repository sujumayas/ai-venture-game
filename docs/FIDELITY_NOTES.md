# Fidelity notes: points to verify against the novel

The game was built from a researched outline (`docs/story-bible.md`) rather than the full text of the book. Each part
was then reviewed by an independent editor against that outline. The items below were flagged as **worth checking
against a physical copy** before the build is shown. None of them changes an outcome of the story.
Invented connecting material is listed per tale in `docs/design/*.md`.

## Frame story (the Consul)
- Theo Lane's antique eyeglasses and Stan Leweski's appearance are not described in the sources used.
- Chronos Keep's interior (murals, stained glass) is a plausible design, not a described one.
- Which items each pilgrim takes from the luggage in the Epilogue (the Consul takes Siri's comlog).
- The Epilogue song is referred to but neither quoted nor played. Its lyrics and melody are still under copyright.
- That it is Hoyt who plays the balalaika at dawn (one source); the 661 steps down from the Keep.
- Kassad's status at dinner ("Retired" in the game, or did he resign his commission?).
- Staging the Consul's Tale as a recording he plays (weakly sourced).
- Which lines of Chaucer Silenus recites at the Yggdrasill dinner (the game uses two lines of the General Prologue).
- When the windwagon reaches Pilgrims' Rest ("by midday" is invented).
- Spanish edition terms for "All Thing" (game: *Asamblea Total*), "CEO" (*Directora Ejecutiva*) and "deathwand" (*varita mortal*).

## The Priest's Tale
- Whether Duré's medscanner survived the Day 110 fire (the game keeps it, so the later scans work).
- Duré addressing the administrator as "M. Orlandi".
- Spanish renderings "Tres Veintenas y Diez" and "¿Eres del cruciforme?" against the published Spanish translation.
- The "Days 215–223" entry merges two failed escape attempts into one.
- Hoyt's remark that people whispered Duré might even become pope (from the novel's own introduction; kept short).

## The Soldier's Tale
- Moneta's hair (short and dark in the game) and whether she offers the name "Mnemosyne" herself.
- The opening line ("up to his knees in mud") and the cadet's "it doesn't simulate, it dreams": check these aren't too
  close to the novel's wording.
- That Kassad wakes near the City of Poets, the hammer at Agincourt, his rank at Bressia, and the title "Lord of Pain".

## The Poet's Tale
- Silenus answers Billy's standing invitation and Billy steps through to fetch him (the novel: Silenus flees to Billy; the game keeps Silenus as the one who reaches out).
- In the courtyard: whether Billy shouts "burn the rest", and how long Silenus hesitates.
- "Mudflat City" (left in English) and "cuna" (crèche) in Spanish.

## The Scholar's Tale
- Whether the dream stops after Sol refuses; Sol speaking of "six" Time Tombs.
- Melio Arundez's visit (weakly sourced; one panel).
- Rachel's age at the accident is deliberately never stated.

## The Detective's Tale
- Whether the Bishop says Johnny volunteered "eight days" earlier.
- Johnny's last word ("Fanny").
- The grey-suited man from the bar being the same man who watches Johnny's rooms (connecting choice).

## The Consul's Tale
- Siri's age at the first meeting, the timing of the reunions (including the sixth, at 70) and her death before the next visit.
- Bertol's fate and the method of the farcaster sabotage (deliberately left vague).
- How long passed between the opening of the Tombs and the Prologue.
- Siri's comlog messages are original writing; the last two hint that she started the rebellion.
- Mike Osho's dying joke about "cutlery": check it isn't too close to the novel.

## Spanish terminology
The game follows terms used in the Spanish editions where known: *Alcaudón* (Shrike), *éxters* (Ousters),
*teleyector* (farcaster), *FUERZA* (FORCE), *TecnoNúcleo*, *Maui-Pacto*, *nave árbol*, *Tumbas del Tiempo*.
Gladstone's Spanish title and other edition-specific terms should be checked against the Spanish translation.
